# 02 — Audiences, Personas & User Journeys

## 3. Target Audience Matrix

Nine audience groups, segmented by **what they are accountable for** — because
accountability, not job title, determines what a visitor needs to see before they
act. A CEO of a 20-person firm and a CEO of a 500-person firm behave differently;
company size is therefore a second axis.

| # | Audience | Accountable for | Primary question | Entry point | Blocking objection | Converting CTA |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | Founder / Owner (SME, 10–50 staff) | Survival and growth | "Will this pay for itself?" | Home, Local pages, Use Cases | "Agencies took my money before" | Book a free consultation |
| A2 | CEO / MD (mid-market, 50–500) | Multi-year growth strategy | "Is this partner credible at my scale?" | Home, About, Work | "Too small to handle us" | Talk to a strategist |
| A3 | CMO / Marketing Head | Pipeline and CAC | "Can they beat my current agency?" | SEO, AI Search, Use Cases | "Can't prove incremental impact" | Request an audit |
| A4 | Marketing Manager / Specialist | Executing the plan | "How does this actually work?" | Blog, Guides, Glossary | "I need to justify this internally" | Download the guide |
| A5 | Sales Head / Revenue Leader | Lead quality and speed-to-contact | "Will this give my team better leads?" | Use Cases, Sales Automation | "More leads, same junk" | Get a lead-flow assessment |
| A6 | Operations / Process Owner | Cost, throughput, error rate | "How much manual work disappears?" | Automation, Use Cases | "Disruption during rollout" | Request an automation assessment |
| A7 | CTO / IT Lead / Product Manager | Architecture, security, delivery risk | "Can they engineer, or just market?" | Technology, Software, Engineering standards | "They're a marketing shop" | Technical discovery call |
| A8 | Procurement / Finance | Cost, terms, risk | "What are we actually buying?" | Engagement models, Pricing, Terms | "Scope will balloon" | Request a proposal |
| A9 | Candidate / Partner | Career or partnership fit | "Is this a serious company?" | Careers, Culture, About | "Small agency, no growth path" | Apply / partner enquiry |

**Design consequence:** A1 and A2 need *reassurance*; A3 and A5 need *comparative
proof*; A4 needs *education*; A6 and A7 need *technical credibility*; A8 needs
*commercial clarity*. One page cannot serve all nine, which is why the site runs
three discovery axes and six CTA tiers rather than a universal "Contact Us."

## 4. User Personas

Eight personas. Each is written from evidence about who actually buys the services
BizzFly lists — sector mix taken from the testimonials currently on the live site
(real estate, education, manufacturing, consulting) and from the service dropdown
on the contact form. Personas are archetypes for design decisions, not claims about
named individuals.

---

### P1 — Meera, Marketing Head at a mid-market SaaS company

**Maps to:** A3 · 34 · Bengaluru · reports to the CEO · team of four

**Goal.** Grow qualified pipeline 40% without growing paid spend. She has noticed
demo requests falling even though rankings held, and suspects AI Overviews are
absorbing her top-of-funnel clicks.

**Pain points.** Organic traffic is flat and click-through is falling. Her current
agency reports rankings, not revenue. She cannot get an answer to "are we showing
up in ChatGPT?" She is out of her depth on the technical side and knows it.

**Questions she asks.**
- Are we cited in AI answers, and how would I even know?
- What is the difference between AEO, GEO and plain SEO — is this a rebrand?
- How long before this shows up in pipeline, honestly?
- Who does the work — a strategist or a junior?

**Objections.** "Every agency now says they do AI search." · "I can't sell a
12-month payback to my CEO." · "We tried an agency, got a deck."

**Desired outcome.** A measurable read on AI and search visibility, and a plan she
can defend in a board meeting.

**Ideal CTA.** *Request an AI visibility assessment* — a scoped diagnostic, not a
sales call. She converts on artefacts, not conversations.

**Journey:** J2 (AI Search)

---

### P2 — Rajesh, Founder of a 30-person manufacturing firm

**Maps to:** A1 + A6 · 48 · Pune · owns the P&L personally

**Goal.** Get more enquiries from outside his existing distributor network, and stop
losing RFQs because his team takes three days to respond.

**Pain points.** His website was built in 2018 and does not appear for the product
terms buyers search. Enquiries arrive by email and get lost. His competitors look
more modern online than they are in reality.

**Questions.** "Will people actually find me?" · "How much does this cost?" · "Do
you understand manufacturing, or will I have to explain my business?" · "Who
maintains it after you build it?"

**Objections.** "I paid an agency ₹X and got nothing." · "This sounds like it needs
a full-time person I don't have." · "Too technical for me."

**Desired outcome.** A steady flow of enquiries he can trace, and less manual
follow-up.

**Ideal CTA.** *Book a free consultation* — he buys from people, after a
conversation. He also responds strongly to the Pune location page, because local
presence is his trust proxy.

**Journey:** J1 (SEO) and J4 (Industry)

---

### P3 — Anita, Operations Director at a hospital group

**Maps to:** A6 · 41 · multi-location healthcare

**Goal.** Cut the load on a front desk drowning in appointment calls and repeat
questions, without hiring.

**Pain points.** 60% of inbound calls are the same six questions. Appointment
no-shows are high. Patient data cannot leave regulated systems, which kills most
off-the-shelf tools.

**Questions.** "Where does patient data go?" · "What happens when the AI is wrong?"
· "Can this connect to our existing system?" · "What does the rollout do to my team
for six weeks?"

**Objections.** "Compliance will block it." · "Our staff won't adopt it." · "A
chatbot will annoy patients."

**Desired outcome.** Measurable reduction in repetitive call volume with a clear
escalation path to a human.

**Ideal CTA.** *Request an automation assessment* — she needs scope and risk
mapped before she can take it to a committee.

**Journey:** J3 (AI Automation)

---

### P4 — Vikram, CTO at a logistics technology company

**Maps to:** A7 · 38 · evaluating a build partner

**Goal.** Ship a customer-facing portal in one quarter without pulling his core team
off the platform roadmap.

**Pain points.** Burned by an outsourcing vendor who delivered unmaintainable code.
Needs a partner who can read an existing codebase, not just greenfield.

**Questions.** "What's your stack, and why?" · "How do you handle handover, testing
and documentation?" · "Who owns the IP?" · "Show me your engineering standards."

**Objections.** "You're a marketing agency with a dev team bolted on." · "Your
case studies are all websites." · "No senior engineer will be on my project."

**Desired outcome.** Confidence that the code will be maintainable by his own team
in a year.

**Ideal CTA.** *Technical discovery call*. He reads `/technology/` and
`/technology/our-engineering-standards/` before he reads anything on Services. If
those pages are thin, he leaves and never sees the rest of the site.

**Journey:** J6 (Engineering evaluation)

---

### P5 — Sneha, Marketing Executive at an education group

**Maps to:** A4 · 26 · two years' experience

**Goal.** Understand what her leadership means by "AI search" so she can brief an
agency and not look lost.

**Pain points.** Everything she reads is either a sales page or unusable jargon.
She has budget influence but no authority.

**Questions.** "What is GEO, in plain terms?" · "What can I do myself?" · "How do I
build a business case?"

**Objections.** She has none — she is not buying. She is *becoming the internal
champion*, which is the most undervalued conversion on a B2B site.

**Desired outcome.** Enough understanding to recommend BizzFly by name.

**Ideal CTA.** *Download the guide* / *Subscribe*. Never a sales call — pushing her
to a demo loses the champion.

**Journey:** J5 (Education-to-champion)

---

### P6 — Prakash, CEO of a real-estate development firm

**Maps to:** A2 · 52 · Mumbai

**Goal.** Compete with organised national developers who outspend him on digital.

**Pain points.** Lead cost has doubled. Portal leads are low quality and shared with
three competitors. Site visits are not converting.

**Questions.** "Who else in real estate have you worked with?" · "What did they
get?" · "Will you work with my existing CRM?" · "Who is accountable to me?"

**Objections.** "Portals already take my budget." · "I need results this quarter."

**Desired outcome.** Direct, owned lead flow that does not depend on portals.

**Ideal CTA.** *Talk to a strategist*. He needs a named senior person, not a form.
He will read the industry page and go straight to case studies. **If Work is empty,
he does not convert** — this persona is the strongest argument for Risk R1.

**Journey:** J4 (Industry) and J7 (High-intent evaluation)

---

### P7 — Deepa, Procurement Manager at a mid-market group

**Maps to:** A8 · 44 · shortlisting three vendors

**Goal.** Compare vendors on a like-for-like basis and de-risk the contract.

**Pain points.** Every agency proposal is structured differently. Scope creep on the
last engagement cost 30% overrun.

**Questions.** "What is in scope and out?" · "What is the engagement model?" ·
"What are payment terms and exit terms?" · "Who is legally contracting?"

**Objections.** "No pricing anywhere on your site." · "Terms are vague."

**Desired outcome.** A comparable, defensible proposal.

**Ideal CTA.** *Request a proposal*. She needs `/engagement-models/` and
`/engagement-models/pricing/` to exist and to be honest, even if the answer is
"ranges, and here's what moves them."

**Journey:** J8 (Procurement)

---

### P8 — Arjun, senior developer considering a job

**Maps to:** A9 · 29 · Pune

**Goal.** Join a company where he will work on real engineering, not template edits.

**Questions.** "What do people actually build here?" · "Is there a growth path?" ·
"Is it a WordPress shop calling itself a tech company?"

**Objections.** "Small agency, no scale." · "Careers page has no open roles."

**Ideal CTA.** *View open roles* — with real role descriptions, not a generic form.

**Journey:** J9 (Careers)

## 5. User Journey Maps

Every journey runs the same five stages — **Discovery → Exploration → Evaluation →
Trust → Conversion** — but the content that satisfies each stage differs sharply by
persona. The design rule: *no stage may be skipped, and no page may serve a stage
it isn't built for.* A service page that tries to do Trust work with a testimonial
carousel is not doing Trust work.

---

### J1 — SEO buyer (P2, P3-adjacent, A1/A3)

| Stage | Where | What must happen |
|---|---|---|
| Discovery | Google → `/services/search-ai-visibility/seo/` or a Local SEO / location page | Answer the query in the first 60 words. No hero-before-answer. |
| Exploration | SEO hub → sub-service (Technical, Local, E-commerce) | Show the *specific* discipline; let them self-select |
| Evaluation | `/services/.../seo/` → Process section → `/process/` | Show what weeks 1–12 look like. Honest timelines beat promises. |
| Trust | → `/work/` filtered to SEO → sector case study | Proof at their scale and sector `[CONTENT REQUIRED]` |
| Conversion | → `/free-seo-audit/` (medium) or `/contact/consultation/` (high) | Two exits: diagnostic for the cautious, call for the ready |

**Exit risk:** a visitor who lands on a sub-service (e.g. Technical SEO) and cannot
get back up to the hub. Mitigated by breadcrumbs and a persistent practice-level
sidebar on all service pages.

---

### J2 — AI search buyer (P1, A3)

| Stage | Where | What must happen |
|---|---|---|
| Discovery | ChatGPT / Perplexity / AI Overview citation, or Google → `/insights/guides/ai-search-optimization-guide/` | The guide must be *citable*: definitions up top, clean headings, FAQ schema |
| Exploration | Guide → `/services/search-ai-visibility/ai-search/` hub | Translate the education into a service without a hard pivot |
| Evaluation | → AEO / GEO / AI Overviews / ChatGPT visibility pages | Distinguish the disciplines credibly; this is where fakers are exposed |
| Trust | → `/insights/topics/ai-search/` body of work + `/technology/ai-governance/` | Depth of published thinking *is* the proof when case studies are thin |
| Conversion | → `/ai-visibility-assessment/` | An artefact, not a call. P1 converts on diagnostics. |

**This is the site's highest-leverage journey.** It is the only one where BizzFly
can win on published expertise before it has a case study library, which makes it
the correct Phase 1 investment.

---

### J3 — Automation buyer (P3, A6)

| Stage | Where | What must happen |
|---|---|---|
| Discovery | Google → `/use-cases/automate-customer-support/` | Lead with the problem in her words, not the technology |
| Exploration | → `/services/ai-automation/` → AI Agents / Chatbots / Support Automation | Show what is automated and what stays human |
| Evaluation | → `/industries/healthcare/` → sector constraints | Address compliance and data residency explicitly, or lose her |
| Trust | → `/technology/security-and-compliance/` + `/technology/ai-governance/` | Failure modes, escalation paths, human-in-the-loop design |
| Conversion | → `/contact/consultation/` scoped as an automation assessment | Scope and risk documented before commitment |

**Exit risk:** the highest on the site. Any page that implies "AI handles it all"
loses her at Evaluation. Automation pages must state what the system will *not* do.

---

### J4 — Industry buyer (P6, P2, A2/A3)

| Stage | Where | What must happen |
|---|---|---|
| Discovery | Google → `/industries/real-estate/` or an industry crossover page | Prove sector fluency in the first screen — use their vocabulary |
| Exploration | Sector problems → mapped services | Connect *their* problem to *our* capability, not a service list |
| Evaluation | → sector use cases → `/work/by-industry/real-estate/` | "Someone like me" is the whole job of this stage |
| Trust | → case study → named client or credible anonymisation | `[CONTENT REQUIRED]` — this journey is blocked without it |
| Conversion | → `/contact/consultation/` with sector pre-filled | Named senior contact, not a generic form |

---

### J5 — Education-to-champion (P5, A4)

| Stage | Where | What must happen |
|---|---|---|
| Discovery | Search / social → blog post or `/insights/glossary/<term>/` | Answer completely; do not gate |
| Exploration | → topic hub → pillar guide | Cluster navigation surfaces the next logical read |
| Evaluation | (of the idea, not the vendor) → comparison pages | `/compare/seo-vs-aeo-vs-geo/` builds her mental model |
| Trust | → author bio, `/about/`, consistency of the body of work | She is judging whether BizzFly *knows things* |
| Conversion | → guide download or newsletter | **Never a sales CTA.** Capture and nurture. |

She converts months later by putting BizzFly on her boss's shortlist. Attribution
will not show this. Design for it anyway.

---

### J6 — Engineering evaluation (P4, A7)

| Stage | Where | What must happen |
|---|---|---|
| Discovery | Referral or direct → `/technology/` | A real stack page, with reasons for choices |
| Exploration | → `/technology/our-engineering-standards/`, `/technology/web-stack/` | Testing, code review, documentation, handover |
| Evaluation | → `/services/software-development/` → relevant sub-service | Delivery model, team composition, seniority |
| Trust | → `/work/` technical case study + `/about/how-we-work/` | Architecture decisions, trade-offs, what went wrong |
| Conversion | → `/contact/technical-discovery/` | Engineer-to-engineer, not a sales qualifier |

**Exit risk:** P4 abandons within 30 seconds if `/technology/` reads like a logo
wall. Technology pages must contain opinions and trade-offs, not badges.

---

### J7 — High-intent evaluation (P6, P1, A2/A3)

Already convinced of the category; choosing between two or three vendors.

Service page → proof → engagement model → pricing signal → FAQ → case study →
consultation.

Requirements: `/engagement-models/` and `/engagement-models/pricing/` must exist and
must not say "contact us for pricing" and nothing else. `/insights/faqs/pricing-and-engagement-faqs/`
answers the awkward questions directly — minimum engagement, what happens if it
doesn't work, notice period, who owns the assets.

---

### J8 — Procurement (P7, A8)

`/engagement-models/` → `/engagement-models/pricing/` → `/terms/` →
`/about/trust-and-security/` → `/get-a-proposal/`.

Deepa does not read marketing pages. She needs scope boundaries, contracting entity,
data handling, and a structured proposal request. Four pages, no hero images. If
these pages don't exist, BizzFly is eliminated in round one of a formal shortlist —
silently, with no enquiry ever received.

---

### J9 — Careers (P8, A9)

`/careers/` → `/careers/life-at-bizzfly/` → `/careers/openings/` → role detail →
apply.

Requirement: `/careers/openings/` must show real roles or state honestly that there
are none open and offer a talent-pool signup. A permanently empty careers page reads
as a dead company to every audience, not just candidates.

---

### Cross-journey rules

1. **Every page belongs to at least one journey.** A page that appears in no journey
   is deleted from the sitemap. This test removed 24 candidate pages during
   architecture.
2. **Every page names its next step.** No dead ends, no "explore more" without a
   destination.
3. **CTA matches stage, never exceeds it.** A Discovery-stage glossary page offering
   "Start a project" converts nothing and costs trust. See document 06, section 17.
4. **The same proof serves multiple journeys.** One real case study is reused across
   its service, its industry, its use case and the Work hub — which is why the case
   study is modelled as a first-class content entity with relationships, not as a
   page (document 05).
